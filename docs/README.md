OctoAcme — Project Management Overview

OctoAcme runs projects with a clear, staged lifecycle that begins with a lightweight initiation (one‑pager, stakeholder alignment, go/no‑go) and progresses through planning, execution, release, and retrospective. Initiation and planning focus on defining measurable success metrics, identifying stakeholders and resources, building a prioritized backlog with acceptance criteria, and maintaining a simple risk register so decisions and timelines are documented in the repository.

Work follows an iterative delivery workflow using a project board (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done) and a disciplined pull request process: keep PRs small when possible, include issue links and acceptance criteria, run automated CI (tests, linting, and security scans) before requesting review, and require at least one approval before merging. Sprint planning pulls work that meets the Definition of Done; backlog items follow a template to ensure clarity around description, acceptance criteria, priority, estimate, and owner.

Roles and communication are defined to ensure ownership and alignment: Product Managers set outcomes and prioritize, Project Managers coordinate delivery and risks, Developers implement and test, QA validates acceptance, and stakeholders provide approvals and direction. The team cadence includes daily standups for tactical updates, weekly delivery syncs for progress and risks, demos at the end of each sprint or milestone, and regular stakeholder updates; escalation paths (team → PM → Product Lead → Sponsor) are documented to resolve blockers quickly.

Quality assurance and risk management are embedded across the lifecycle: unit and integration tests, end‑to‑end smoke tests for critical flows, security scanning in CI, and manual QA as needed. Releases follow a pre‑release checklist (acceptance criteria met, passing CI, rollback plan, smoke tests) with templates for release notes and incident playbooks. Retrospectives capture action items for the backlog and the risk register is reviewed regularly to keep stakeholders informed.
